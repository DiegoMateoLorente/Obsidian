# Grammar
## Prepositions with time
### At
- Precise time:
	- 5 O'clock: I will be there at 5 O'clock
	- Midnight: I have to go there at midnight
	- Lunch time: She left at lunch time
	- Present: I am okay at present
- Or:
	- Weekend: Will we meet at weekend?
	- Christmas: I'm going to be with my family at Christmas
	- Night: We will go to the party at night
	- The moment: At the moment I don't want that
	- The same time: We were walking on that park at the same time
### In
- Longer periods:
	- Months:
		- January: I will go to Madrid in January
	- Seasons:
		- Winter: Zaragoza is so cold in winter
	- Years
		- 2026: I will be working in 2026
	- The past: I woult like to be in the past
	- The 21st century: Right now we are living in the 21st century
- After some time:
	- Seven seconds: I will be out of the school in seven seconds
	- Nine months: We will have our child in 9 months
	- Six days'time: I'm going to be in Madrid in six days'time
- Or:
	- The morning: I was at university in the morning
	- The afternoon: I go to play football in the afternoon
	- The evening: He has a shower in the evening
### On
- Days:
	- Monday: The meeting is on Monday
	- Tuesday: I have class on Tuesday
- Dates:
	- 21st June 2023 (all the date fields are necesary except the year): It will happen on 21st June 2023
	- 15th May: The wedding is on 15th May
### No prepositions
- Last year: I was in Canarias last year
- Next Friday: I will be there next friday
- Next weekend: We will meet next weekend
- This month: I will go to the gym this month
- Every Thursday: I go to the park every Thursday
## Some adjectives with prepositions
### At
We use “at” with adjectives like good/bad/amazing/brilliant/terrible, etc. to talk about skills and abilities. Examples:
- He's really good at English. 
- She's amazing at the piano. 
- They're terrible at organising anything. 
- I'm not very good at drawing
### About
We often use “about” with adjectives of feelings like angry/excited/happy/nervous/sad/stressed/worried, etc. to explain what is causing that feeling
- I'm angry about the decision. 
- He's nervous about the presentation. 
- She's excited about the new job. 
- They were worried about the exam.
### Of
However, sometimes we use “of” with feelings.
- She was afraid of telling her mum. 
- I'm frightened of having an accident. 
- He's scared of flying. 
- You should be proud of your progress.
### To
We can use “to” to show the connection between people or things.
- He's married to the director. 
- I'm addicted to my phone. 
- I'm allergic to nuts. 
- It's similar to the old one.
We can also use “to” to talk about someone's behaviour towards someone else.
- They were really friendly to me. 
- Was he nice to you? 
- He is always polite to everyone. 
- She was very rude to the waitress
### For
- Exercise is good for you. 
- Stress is bad for you. 
- The town is famous for its cheese. 
- I'm responsible for the financial side of the business.
### In
- She's interested in the project. 
- They want someone who's experienced in design. 
- I didn't want to get involved in the argument.
